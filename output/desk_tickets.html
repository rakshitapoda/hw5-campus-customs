1. How well the agents performed on each ticket

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee good

The agents got the hard part right. Inventory found size S at 0, and instead of stopping there it checked vendors and noticed that Bulldog Print Co had can_ship = false because of invoice #501. That's the dependency the whole ticket hinged on. Accounting then prepared both the $840 invoice payment and the $8 PO in one step. It passed pending_total=840 to the PO call so the cash check counted the invoice too, and the PO correctly came back blocked. After I approved the invoice and reran the ticket, Accounting didn't try to pay #501 again. It only prepared the PO, which is now awaiting approval (arrival 2026-09-05), and after that approval the ticket resolved on its own.

What kept it from being great: it needed two runs (12 + 13 model calls, about 59k tokens) for a $8 t-shirt order. The agents also re-checked things a lot. get_ticket(101) was called 6 times across the two runs, and Accounting called check_vendor_status even though Inventory had just reported the vendor status in its task. The second customer draft also told Tauhid that "a restock order is awaiting approval and has not been placed yet". That's accurate, but it's more of our internal process than a customer needs to hear.

Ticket 102: Rent due best

This was the cleanest run: 10 model calls, 10 tool calls, about 20.5k tokens, done in 27 seconds. The Boss sent it to Facilities, Facilities called get_lease(1) and confirmed the email matched the lease (Elm City Properties, $2,400, due 2026-09-02, 2 days after the shop date), and then Facilities delegated to Accounting itself, so the Boss didn't have to. Accounting prepared the rent payment, I approved it, and execute_payment dropped checking from $2,552 to $152 and moved the lease's next_due to 2026-10-02.

It went well because the ticket is basically one lookup plus one payment with no judgment involved. It wasn't always this smooth, though. In an earlier test run (Problem 5), Accounting refused to prepare the rent because it "wasn't overdue yet", and I had to change the prompt to say that preparing early is fine because a human still approves it.

Ticket 103: Bulk hoodie discount mixed, but the right outcome

The final outcome was correct. The agents didn't invent a discount: Accounting checked $52.20, $49.30, and $46.40 (10/15/20% off) with check_margin, confirmed none were below the $22 cost, and the Boss flagged needs_human, which is exactly what should happen with a pricing decision. The prepare_purchase_order guardrail also worked as designed: the 12-unit restock ($264) was refused because only $152 was left after 101 and 102. Cash never went negative. After I recorded my decision (10% off on the 8 in stock, no restock), the second run took only 10 model calls, and Customer Service wrote a good reply offering the 8 hoodies at $52.20.

The first run was messy, though. It was the most expensive run of the whole project (20 model calls, 50,272 tokens). The Boss delegated to Accounting twice (once for margins, then again for the restock), Accounting made a pointless probe call with proposed_unit_price=0, and in run 2 it checked the margin for qty 20 instead of the 8 we were actually selling. The per-unit margin is the same, so it didn't change anything, but it shows the agents weren't fully tracking the "only 8" decision.

3. What would have been simpler with one agent using tools

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. It's a lookup and a payment. One agent with get_ticket, get_lease, get_cash_balance, and prepare_payment could do it in about four tool calls. With the team, it took three agents, two delegations, and 10 model calls, and the ticket got read three separate times.

The repeated context-passing was overhead. Across the 5 runs there were 65 tool calls, and 15 of them were delegate_task and 16 were get_ticket. So almost half the tool calls were agents handing off work or re-reading a ticket another agent had already read. Only 5 calls (prepare_payment ×2, prepare_purchase_order ×3) actually produced anything that changed money.

The thin agents didn't really need to be agents. Facilities only has one real tool (get_lease), and Customer Service's job is basically "write a message from facts someone else found". Both could have been a single tool call or a final step of one agent's run.

Cash coordination was harder because of multiple agents. Because different agents could prepare payments in different runs, I had to build a shared proposals list, a pending_total parameter, a "don't prepare duplicates" note, de-duplication, and an output validator that rejects any payment not returned by a tool. In Problem 5 an agent even misread its own proposal as a "duplicate". One agent with one conversation would naturally remember what it had already prepared.

That said, splitting into agents did help in some ways. Only Accounting has the prepare_* tools, which is an easy safety boundary to explain, and the dashboard's agent floor makes it really clear who did what. For a shop this small, though, a single agent with a well-scoped tool list (plus the same human approval gate) would have been simpler, cheaper, and probably just as accurate.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs could handle with what we already built

  1. Restocking the Crest Mug. CC-MUG-CREST (OS) is at 0 in inventory. Inventory would find it with check_inventory, and check_vendor_status would show Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3 lead days) with no open invoice, so can_ship = true. Accounting could prepare_purchase_order at $3.50 each, and a small order fits in the $152 we have left (for example 20 mugs = $70). It would go through the same approval tray, and execute_purchase_order would record it and debit cash.
  2. A discount request on the Yale Cap. Same flow as ticket 103: check_inventory("CC-HAT-BLUE", "OS") shows 40 in stock, so there's no shortage this time. Accounting runs check_margin against the $6 cost and $22 list price, the Boss flags needs_human, I record the discount through the decision box, and Customer Service drafts the reply. No money moves, so it would probably resolve in two short runs.
  3. October's rent notice. After our run, lease #1's next_due is 2026-10-02. If Elm City Properties sent another notice, Facilities would verify it with get_lease (32 days until due), and Accounting's prepare_payment("rent", 1) would come back refused, because $152 can't cover $2,400. The system can already catch that and flag it for a human instead of overdrawing the account, which is exactly what the shop rules want.

5. Three new problems Campus Customs can't handle yet, and what it would need

  1. Actually selling the 8 hoodies to Yale AI Club. Ticket 103 is "resolved", but nothing in the database shows the sale. There's no sales or orders table, no tool to reduce inventory.qty from 8 to 0, and no way to record the $417.60 coming in (8 × $52.20), because this assignment only lets cash flow out. It would need a sales table, a record_sale(sku, size, qty, unit_price) tool that decrements stock and credits cash (with human approval), and probably a Sales agent or an extension of Customer Service to handle the order.
  2. Receiving the tee when it arrives on 2026-09-05. We paid $8 for the tee PO, but execute_purchase_order deliberately doesn't change stock, and nothing tracks that an order is on the way. Size S is still 0 in inventory, and desk.date_today is stuck at 2026-08-31. It would need a purchase_orders table with statuses (ordered → received), a receive_shipment(po_id) tool that adds the units to inventory, and a way to advance the shop date, so Inventory could then close the loop and have Customer Service tell Tauhid the tee is ready.
  3. Handling replies and new requests on their own. Customer Service's draft to Yale AI Club ends with "let us know if you'd like to proceed with the 8", but the system has no way to receive that answer. Agents can't create tickets, drafts are never sent, and nothing reads incoming email. It would need an inbox tool that turns incoming messages into tickets (create_ticket), a send step that only goes out after a human approves the draft (just like payments), and a Boss that can link a reply to the original ticket. I'd also add a cash-forecast tool, since the shop has $152 and $2,400 of rent due in October. Right now no agent can warn about that ahead of time; they only find out when a payment gets refused.
